# So, who actually does the sourcing?

Every sourcing tool ends the same way: it hands you a list of suppliers — and then the real work starts, and it's yours. The emails. The WeChat threads at 6 AM. The quote in a screenshot of a spreadsheet. The carton math that doesn't add up.

So we built a sourcing employee, not a search engine.

It starts day one trained on 4M+ vetted suppliers, curated by the world's leading sourcing experts. You message it like a person — WhatsApp, WeChat, Slack, Telegram, SMS, or email — and it runs the whole job: RFQ, factory outreach, normalized quotes, sample payments, PO, inspection, freight, documents. Every consequential step comes back to you for a yes.

So far, 2,300+ factory conversations have run through it — real factories, real quotes, real shipments.

## What it does that a search tool can't

- **Emails factories.** Real outreach in their language, on email and WeChat, chased until they answer. You're cc'd on everything.
- **Compares quotes.** Three currencies, three formats, one honest table: unit price, MOQ, lead time, notes.
- **Issues POs.** Cuts the purchase order on your terms — 30% deposit, 70% against B/L — and sends it.
- **Pays for samples.** Sample invoice paid by PayPal, receipt filed to the job automatically.
- **Sends a human.** AI can't walk a factory floor, so it books SGS, QIMA or Bureau Veritas to do it — then reads the report.
- **Books the freight.** Live rates, booking, tracking, and the documents that clear customs.

## Examples of what it has actually caught

- **The missing certification.** "This product needs FCC for US import." Caught before the shipment left, not after it was seized.
- **The carton math.** Client spec said 24 pcs/carton; the factory packed 12. The shipment would've been blocked at the warehouse.
- **The quiet factory.** Factory C went silent for three days. It chased — politely, in Chinese, at Shenzhen business hours.
- **The quote spread.** Listed price $11.20/unit. Real factory quotes: $8.90, $9.40, $9.85. That's −21% found in one RFQ round.
- **The QC contradiction.** The inspection report said 500 units passed; the PO said 550 shipped. Flagged before the invoice was paid.
- **The 2 AM "book it."** The most common owner reply, across every channel: two words, sent late, acted on by morning in Asia.

## Common questions

- **Which channels does it work on?** WhatsApp, WeChat, Slack, Telegram, SMS (US numbers), and email. Factory-side, it works where factories actually reply: email and WeChat. Machine-side: MCP and Agent2Agent.
- **Does it really contact factories?** Yes. Real outreach, in the factory's language, chased until answered. You're cc'd on everything.
- **Can it really pay for things?** Sample invoices, yes — by PayPal, with your approval and a receipt filed to the job. Larger payments stay with you.
- **Is my data safe?** Your catalog, specs, and quotes stay in your workspace. Factories see only what an RFQ needs. We never resell your pricing data.
- **What does it cost?** Your first RFQ is free. After that, plans scale with order volume — and service bookings (inspection, testing, freight) are pay-per-use with the price shown before you approve.
- **How fast are quotes?** Factories typically reply in 2–3 days. Everything normalizes into one comparable table the moment replies land.
- **My company already built its own purchasing tool. Can it use this?** Yes — that's what the MCP connector is for. Your tool keeps its interface; ours does the sourcing underneath.
- **What if it gets something wrong?** Every catch and every commitment routes through a human approval. If a factory reply is ambiguous, it says so instead of guessing.
- **Who's behind this?** People who do this work. It's built and tested daily on live orders at Headwind, a real sourcing operation.
- **Where does the supplier data come from?** 4M+ manufacturers across 200+ countries of verified trade data, sharpened by every executed order: real quotes vs listed prices, true lead times, QC pass rates, factory response behavior.
